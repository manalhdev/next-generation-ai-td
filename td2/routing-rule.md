Built only from the 100 real TD 1 cells: rates in [results/family-rates-td2.md](../results/family-rates-td2.md), generated by `tools/td2_family_rates.py`. Twenty questions support a hypothesis for this batch, not a general error rate, a permanent ranking or a quorum guarantee.

## Uses and cost of an error

| Use | Consequence of a wrong figure | Checking principle |
|---|---|---|
| Internal dashboard | Seen internally, corrected later | `none` only with a model at 100 % on the family in TD 1 |
| Figure shown to a customer | Wrong information to a customer, loss of trust | Quorum always |
| Amount paid out | Money paid on a wrong amount | Deterministic reference query and human review, always |

## Portfolio basis

All five TD 1 routes are free (price 0: Groq Free plan for gpt-oss-120b, gpt-oss-20b, qwen3.8-27b, portfolio 2026-09-28; OpenRouter `:free` for Cohere and Dots, absent from the portfolio). The only documented paid route of a TD 1 model is `openrouter:openai/gpt-oss-120b` at 0.15 / 0.60 USD per million tokens (portfolio, read 2026-09-29): with the TD 1 median of gpt-oss-120b (638 input, 182.5 output tokens), about 0.00021 USD per call. As every candidate costs 0, the tie is broken by keeping gpt-oss-120b for the families where it is the only model at 100 %, so gpt-oss-20b goes first wherever it is at 100 %.

## What changed in `atelier-routage.py`

- `RESOURCES`: the three fictitious resources are replaced by the five TD 1 routes (free) and the paid route of gpt-oss-120b.
- Eligibility: the abstract `level >= difficulty` test is replaced by "correct on every TD 1 cell of the family", read from `model-question-comparison-en.csv`; each reason cites the cells.
- `checks`: depend on the use (dashboard, customer, payout) and on the family, as in the table above.
- The order of the branches is unchanged: included capacity, then deferral, then paid route, then abstention. Among eligible free routes, the order follows the rule (20B, Cohere, Dots, 120B, Qwen) instead of `(allocation, level, id)`.

## Priority order in the supplied `choose`

`choose` tests its branches in a fixed order and returns at the first one that applies:

1. `included`: eligible resources that are available and cost no new cash (`cash == 0`). An available subscription (`cash == 0`, `allocation > 0`) belongs here, like a free route, because it is already paid.
2. `waiting`: eligible resources that are not available, cost no cash and refill before `deadline_minutes`, only if the request can wait.
3. `paid`: available routes whose cash fits the budget.
4. Abstention.

So an available subscription is selected at step 1, and the function never reaches step 2, where it would defer to wait for an exhausted free resource. The reasons: the subscription is already paid, so using it spends no new money; it answers now instead of after a delay; and a deferral is uncertain, because a reset time is only an announcement (the code says availability must be measured again at wake-up) and the deadline can be missed. Inside step 1, the key `(allocation, level, id)` still prefers a free route (allocation 0) to the subscription (0.04) when both are available.

The priority should be reversed when the subscription capacity is scarce and has a cost of opportunity: its allocation for the period is limited and must be kept for requests that cannot wait (urgent, difficult or customer-facing ones), or each use counts against a cap shared by the team or triggers overage. Then, for a request that can wait and whose free refill comes reliably before the deadline, deferring keeps the subscription for the urgent requests. In the code, the `waiting` branch would be tested before the subscription when `request["can_wait"]` is true and the remaining subscription allocation is under a reserve threshold.

In our adapted rule there is no subscription; the same order means that, on F2, the simulator waits for the free Groq refill before paying the OpenRouter route, which matches the zero-spend constraint of the course.
